# Research: Development Methodologies

## What is Agile?

Agile is a way of developing software based on collaboration, quick adaptation, and continuous delivery of value. Instead of working for months without showing results, Agile proposes dividing the work into small parts in order to deliver frequent progress and receive constant feedback.

The main goal of Agile is to create software more efficiently by responding quickly to changes and maintaining close communication between the team and the client.

Agile methodologies aim to:

* Improve communication between team members.
* Adapt easily to changing requirements.
* Deliver functional versions of the product continuously.
* Reduce errors through frequent reviews.
* Increase customer satisfaction.

Agile is not a single methodology, but a set of principles and practices. Some well-known Agile methodologies are Scrum and Kanban.

---

## What is Scrum?

Scrum is an Agile methodology used to organize and manage software development projects. It works through short work cycles called *sprints*, where the team develops a functional part of the product.

Scrum helps teams work in an organized and collaborative way with clear objectives.

### Main Scrum Concepts

### Scrum Roles

#### Product Owner

The Product Owner is responsible for defining what the product needs and deciding priorities. This person represents the interests of the client or users.

#### Scrum Master

The Scrum Master ensures that the team correctly follows the Scrum methodology. This role also helps remove obstacles that affect the team's work.

#### Development Team

The development team is the group of people who design, program, test, and deliver the software.

---

### Sprint

A sprint is a short work period, usually between one and four weeks. During this time, the team develops specific tasks to create a functional version of the product.

At the end of each sprint, the results are reviewed and the next cycle is planned.

---

### Product Backlog

The Product Backlog is an organized list of all tasks, features, and improvements required for the project. The Product Owner decides which items are the highest priority.

---

### Sprint Backlog

The Sprint Backlog is the set of tasks selected from the Product Backlog that the team will complete during the current sprint.

---

### Sprint Review

A Sprint Review is a meeting held at the end of the sprint to present the completed work and receive feedback.

---

### Sprint Retrospective

A Sprint Retrospective is a meeting where the team analyzes what went well, what problems appeared, and how to improve in the next sprint.

---

## What is Kanban?

Kanban is a visual work methodology used to organize tasks and improve workflow. Its goal is to help teams clearly see which tasks are pending, in progress, or completed.

Kanban usually uses a board divided into columns, for example:

* To Do
* In Progress
* In Review
* Done

Each task is represented by a card that moves between columns according to its status.

### How is Kanban used?

Kanban is used to:

* Visualize work progress.
* Organize tasks in a simple way.
* Prevent the team from handling too many tasks at the same time.
* Detect delays or blocked tasks quickly.
* Continuously improve workflow.

Unlike Scrum, Kanban does not use sprints or fixed time periods. Tasks are completed continuously.

---

## Differences Between Scrum and Kanban

| Scrum                                                     | Kanban                                      |
| --------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------- |
| Works with fixed-length sprints.                          | Work flows continuously.                    |
| Has defined roles such as Scrum Master and Product Owner. | Does not require specific roles.            |
| Work is planned before each sprint.                       | Tasks can be added at any time.             |
| Includes frequent and structured meetings.                | Has fewer rules and meetings are optional.  |
| Focuses on delivering results at the end of each sprint.  | Focuses on maintaining a constant workflow. |
| More structured.                                          | More flexible.                              |

---

## When Should Scrum Be Used?

Scrum is recommended when:

* The project is large or complex.
* Strong team organization is needed.
* There are clear short-term goals.
* The client requires frequent reviews.
* Constant planning is necessary.

For example, Scrum can be used in the development of applications, web platforms, or business systems.

---

## When Should Kanban Be Used?

Kanban is recommended when:

* Work changes constantly.
* Flexibility is required.
* The team receives tasks continuously.
* Visual organization is important.
* Workflow control is necessary.

For example, Kanban can be used in technical support teams, software maintenance, or content management.

---

## Conclusion

Agile is a work philosophy focused on collaboration, adaptation, and continuous value delivery. Within Agile, there are methodologies such as Scrum and Kanban, each with different characteristics.

Scrum offers a more organized structure through sprints and defined roles, while Kanban focuses on visualization and continuous task flow. The choice between one methodology or the other depends on the needs of the project and the way the team works.
